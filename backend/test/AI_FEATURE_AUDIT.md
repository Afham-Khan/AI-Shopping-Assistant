## Notes

<!-- notes:start -->
BEFORE was measured with the same audit script against the committed code (`git archive HEAD`), AFTER against the working tree, so both columns use identical checks. This block is kept when the report is regenerated.

**What was fixed (general mechanisms, no per-query rules)**

- **Shared shopper rules — `engine/complements.js` (new).** Who an item is for (gender, children's items detected from the title and "Ideal For"), novelty props (`Use: costume…`), accessory kinds (audio, speaker, case, power, charger, camera, studio), device compatibility (a case has to name the phone; Apple-only gear such as MagSafe, Apple Watch or HomePod doesn't go with Samsung, Oppo or Asus; Bluetooth audio works with anything), substitutes (another phone, perfume or pair of shoes is not "the rest of the set") and a stricter "goes with" table. Complete the look, cart insights, the gift finder and bundles all use it.
- **Compare.** Spec rows now pick the winner on the headline figure ("64 GB (microSD up to 256 GB)" counts as 64 GB; "5 h, 24 h with case" counts as 5 h). Before, the biggest number in the string won. A product under 3.5★ is no longer listed as both "Highest rated" and "Lower rating".
- **Bundle.** Keywords match whole words. Roles can say what kind of accessory they need (so "Earphones" can't be a "wireless" charger and "Power bank" can't be a MagSafe *case*), which titles are excluded (a bedside table is not a desk) and which specs matter (explainer levels, e.g. graphics for gaming). A `must` rule means a gaming laptop has a dedicated GPU whenever an affordable one exists. Children's items, costume props and phone cases never fill another role. Party/wedding outfits prefer dressy pieces (Occasion/Style). Ratings now count more than raw sales. Pieces the catalog doesn't stock are explained as unavailable, not as "left out to stay within budget".
- **Gift finder.** Children get children's things (a girls' dress, sports gear, tablets, headphones) and adults never get children's clothing. Cases, chargers, power banks, studio gear and costume props are never gifts. Selfie and camera gear only appear with a photo, travel or tech interest. If the recipient's gender is unknown (boss, colleague), neutral items are preferred. A product made for the interest (a gamer tee, headphones for gaming) beats one that only shares the broad category. The occasion now affects the score, and the budget-use weight was raised so a Rs 20,000 gift isn't a Rs 1,949 pair of tongs. When only one category fits, the list is filled up instead of showing 2–3 ideas.
- **Complete the look.** A child's dress gets only inexpensive, child-friendly add-ons (no watches, fragrance, jewellery or heels). Adult looks never include children's items. A unisex base (sunglasses) follows one gender instead of mixing a men's tee, a women's bag and a girls' dress. Non-fashion items ("goes well with") keep only bought-together picks that really go with the product, then fill from compatible complements (phone → its charger, earbuds, wireless charger; laptop → audio; blender → kitchen tools and ingredients).
- **Cart insights.** Complete-the-set uses the same filters: compatible with every device in the cart, not another version of something already in it, and matching the cart's gender. The free-shipping gap now uses the cart's own rule (subtotal minus coupon discount, `FREE_SHIPPING_THRESHOLD` / `SHIPPING_FEE` constants) instead of a hard-coded 5000 / Rs 250 that ignored coupons.
- **Spec explainer.** A low-res HD+ screen is no longer called "great for videos and games". Battery titles keep the Wh figure.
- **Product Q&A.** Yes/no answers say "yes" only when the asked feature is actually in the passage. A contrary value gives "No" (automatic → "6-speed manual", wireless → "5W wired", real leather → "PU faux", 5G → "LTE"), splash-proof gives "Only partly", and anything else is "The listing doesn't mention that", never a blind "Yes". New use-case answers ("is it good for gaming / photography / travel / work?") are judged from the explainer levels, so a laptop without a GPU gets "only light games". "How long / how big / how heavy" are treated as lookups. Spec rows beat highlights. New synonyms cover longevity, transmission, Face ID/Touch ID and stylus. Price, stock and rating answers now cite evidence.
- **Review summary.** The lexicon now catches complaints it used to read as neutral or positive ("feels cheap", "poor value for money", "not what I expected", "mushy", "heats up", "disappears too quickly"). Negation stops at a comma. "Okay" counts as lukewarm. A pro needs one clearly positive remark or several mild ones. The star average caps the label: about 4★ for "Very positive", 3.2★ for "Positive", and below 2.5★ it's "Negative".
- **Shopping DNA.** Breadth is now the evenness of activity across categories (normalized entropy), not a count of categories touched once. "Curious Explorer" is damped when one department dominates: the demo user, 67% electronics, is now a Tech Enthusiast instead of a Curious Explorer. Price sensitivity counts more, so a shopper buying the cheapest item in each category is a Smart Saver.
- **Similar products (`ranking/recommender.js` → `similar`).** Candidates must be for the same audience (`wrongGender` / children's items from `engine/complements.js`) and the same kind of product: same category first, then the same department with a shared product type (catalog tag or `attributes.js` type), and another category in the department only when the embedding similarity is at least 0.6. Embedding similarity (with MMR) still orders each tier; a closer tier always comes first, and the list is left short rather than padded (the motorcycle gets its 3 bikes, not a car). Before, the department and category were only small score bonuses.
- **Bought together (`ranking/recommender.js` → `boughtTogether`).** The association rules (now `associationRules`) are only a signal: every pick goes through `complementaryFor(base, catalog, { limit, seed: rules })`, i.e. `goesWith` (compatible with the device, same audience, not a substitute, the same sport) and at most 2 per category / accessory kind, then fills from the best complementary products. When nothing genuinely goes with the item the result is empty, so the rail is hidden (the office chair gets 2 decor pieces, the motorcycle 2 sunglasses). The old raw same-category penalty and the `L.COMPLEMENTS` fill (which let phones, tablets and studio gear in) are gone. `dialogue/manager.js` uses the same logic for non-fashion "what goes with this" ("These go well with …") and says so honestly when nothing fits, instead of claiming "shoppers often add these".

**Changed by someone else:** the guided finder's "fruits" case (Juice in the top 3) passes now because of the concurrent retrieval changes in `engine/retrieval/**`. `finder.js` was not changed.

**Still failing — needs changes outside this scope**

- *Snap & Find (`visual.js`, unchanged)*: recall on cropped, mirrored "phone photos" of 49 thumbnails is top-1 product 37/49, top-3 41/49, right category 45/49. Two produce shots (Mulberry, Volleyball) are rejected as "a screenshot" by the open-set rule, and two pantry items (Red Onions, Tissue Paper Box) are detected as Home Decor. These need a recalibration (`node scripts/calibrateVisual.js`) with augmented positives, or better prompts for the Fresh & Pantry class. Not changed here, to avoid moving the calibrated thresholds without a fresh calibration run.

**What the catalog can't support** (the features now say so honestly instead of substituting): no desk or study table (the home-office bundle reports it isn't stocked); no formal men's shoes (wedding looks fall back to the cleanest sneakers); no dedicated gaming laptop under Rs 504k (the RTX 2060 Zenbook is the only one); no children's accessories, shoes or toys (kids' gifts and looks are limited to the girls' dress, sports gear and headphones); no men's fragrance except the unisex CK One; no men's watch between Rs 25k and Rs 420k.
